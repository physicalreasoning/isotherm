# G3 · Synthetic pretraining

Does pretraining LadderNet on simulated ladders, built from real forecasts and real outcomes at
stations Kalshi does not list, make it better or more data-efficient on the real market? This is
the one ingredient of the Jev recipe (training on synthetic data) the model did not have yet.
Scope: the 16:00 day-before read, the one the frozen strategy trades, which needs no intraday
observations.

## Method

**Physics corpus.** 55 US airports across climates (`isotherm.pretrain.STATIONS`), none of them a
Kalshi settlement station (KNYC, KMDW, KMIA, KAUS, KLAX, KDEN, KPHL are excluded and a test locks
that in). For each: NWS CLI highs, GFS MOS and NBM from IEM, 2021 onward. Causal EMOS and
climatology fits exactly as for the real cities (monthly refits, 2-day embargo), so every synthetic
row carries the same inputs LadderNet sees on Kalshi.

**Ladders.** Kalshi's own layout: 94% of real day-before ladders are two tails plus four 2°F
buckets spanning 8°F. Synthetic ladders copy that, centred on the NBM EMOS mean plus an offset
drawn from Kalshi's real centring offsets (median −0.6°F). The offsets are a listing convention
and carry no outcome information.

**Market simulator.** The real market is much sharper than any public forecast (its log score beats
EMOS-NBM by about 0.12 nats at this read), so a crowd that only reads forecasts would teach the
model the wrong lesson. The simulated crowd anchors on the NBM EMOS posterior and also sees a
private signal of the true high with noise τ, then adds logit noise λ. τ and λ are fit so the
simulated market matches the real one on two numbers: its log score gap to EMOS-NBM and its own
calibration (the weight of the tempered market). Overround, spreads and traded volume are
resampled from real rows.

**Causality.** Same-day weather at a neighbouring station is close to the answer, so every
walk-forward fold refits the simulator on that fold's real training rows only and pretrains only
on synthetic days up to the fold's last training day.

**Training.** Mini-batch log-score pretraining on the synthetic ladders, then the usual LadderNet
fine-tune on real rows from the pretrained weights and feature normalisation
(`LadderNet(init_states=..., init_stats=...)`; defaults leave the original training untouched).
With too few real rows to fine-tune (under 200), the pretrained nets are used as they are, where
the plain model falls back to the market.

## Gate (PLAN §6), made operational before the run

Learning curves at 10/25/50/100% of real training dates, with and without pretraining, 3 seeds per
arm, identical walk-forward rows, Δ log score vs the market with date-block 95% CIs.

- (a) **same score with half the data:** pretrained on 50% minus none on 100% has a point estimate
  ≥ 0 and a CI lower bound above −0.005 nats; or
- (b) **better with all the data:** pretrained on 100% minus none on 100% has a CI lower bound above 0.

Control: pretrain on labels sampled from the simulated market and fine-tune on labels sampled from
the real market. It must score like the market.

## Results (2026-10-05)

`scripts/g3_ablation.py`; raw numbers in `results/g3_learning_curve.json`.

**Corpus.** 92,452 synthetic day-before ladders from all 55 stations (none skipped; about 2,100 CLI
days each), 87,066 of them before the lockbox. That is about 12 times the 7,686 real ladders.

**Simulator check.** Fit on real pre-lockbox ladders, applied to the pre-lockbox corpus:

| | Real | Simulated |
|---|---:|---:|
| market minus EMOS-NBM, log score | −0.109 | −0.110 |
| market log score | 1.372 | 1.367 |
| tempered-market weight | 1.18 | 1.13 |
| median overround | 1.050 | 1.065 |
| median spread | 0.045 | 0.040 |
| market top-1 accuracy | 0.405 | 0.403 |

Fitted private-signal noise τ = 5.0°F and logit noise λ = 0. Refit per fold, τ ranges from about 5
to 29°F: the early folds, when the real market was barely sharper than EMOS, get a nearly
uninformed simulated crowd, as they should.

**Learning curves.** 6,063 walk-forward ladders, 12 quarterly folds, Δ log score vs the market:

| Real training data | No pretraining | Pretrained | Pretrained minus none |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10% | +0.0183 [+0.0124, +0.0244] | +0.0356 [+0.0292, +0.0422] | **+0.0173 [+0.0128, +0.0218]** |
| 25% | +0.0361 [+0.0282, +0.0440] | +0.0378 [+0.0310, +0.0448] | +0.0018 [−0.0020, +0.0053] |
| 50% | +0.0429 [+0.0344, +0.0519] | +0.0366 [+0.0297, +0.0438] | −0.0064 [−0.0108, −0.0019] |
| 100% | +0.0453 [+0.0368, +0.0541] | +0.0407 [+0.0342, +0.0474] | −0.0047 [−0.0090, −0.0005] |

- (a) pretrained on 50% minus none on 100%: −0.0087 [−0.0133, −0.0046]. **Fails.**
- (b) pretrained minus none at 100%: −0.0047 [−0.0090, −0.0005]. **Fails.**
- Last 12 months only, pretrained minus none at 100%: +0.0037 [−0.0010, +0.0085], positive but not
  distinguishable from zero.
- Control: +0.0046 [+0.0039, +0.0054].

## Verdict: G3 fails, with one useful exception

Pretraining on simulated ladders does not make LadderNet better or more data-efficient once it
has a few thousand real ladders. With 50% or 100% of the real data it is slightly *worse* than
training on real ladders alone, by about 0.005 nats, and the gate fails on both clauses. As
pre-registered, pretraining is not added to the model.

The exception is the data-poor end. With 10% of the real training dates (roughly 90 to 650
ladders per fold) pretraining adds +0.017 nats, nearly doubling the gain over the market. That
makes it worth keeping as a tool for new contracts with little history, such as a
city Kalshi has just listed or the daily-low and rain markets, rather than for the seven cities
we already have three years of.

**The control is not exactly zero.** Trained on market-sampled labels at both stages it scores
+0.0046 [+0.0039, +0.0054] over the market, where the same control without pretraining sat
within ±0.002. Some of the pretrained arms' gain, up to about 0.005 nats, may therefore come from
the pretraining stage itself rather than from real outcomes. That does not rescue the verdict (it
can only make the pretrained arms look better than they are, and they already lose at 50% and
100%), and the +0.017 at 10% is well above it, but it is the first thing to understand before
pretraining is used anywhere.

**Why it does not help more.** The simulator reproduces the market's sharpness, calibration,
overround and spreads, but not *why* the real crowd is sharp. Its crowd sees a noisy copy of the
truth; the real crowd knows things about these seven stations (local effects, what the NWS
discussion says) that no other station teaches. Once there is enough real data, that specific
knowledge is what matters, and simulated stations dilute it.
